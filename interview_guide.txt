Interview storyteller process with all steps and questions
1. 13 Perception Change Factors and Linked Storytelling Steps
We'll align each perception change factor with a relevant step in a 5-step storytelling process to create a coherent narrative flow for the interview.
Perception Change Factors:
1.	Non-disabled People Working Closely with Disabled People → Moment, Details, Change
2.	Focusing on Similarities Instead of Differences → Moment, Details
3.	Providing New Knowledge about Disability, Including Ableism → Realization, Change
4.	Getting to Know the Person Behind the Disability → Details, Realization
5.	Time and Opportunity for Reflexivity → Moment, Change, Resolution
6.	Moments of Realization and Awareness → Moment, Realization
7.	Engaging with Provocative Art → Moment, Details, Realization
8.	Witnessing a Disabled Person Demonstrating Their Capabilities → Details, Realization, Change
9.	Co-production and Inclusion of Disabled People in Leadership Roles → Moment, Details, Change
10.	Challenging Self-Limiting Beliefs in Disabled People → Moment, Change
11.	Disability Awareness and Inclusivity Training → Realization, Change
12.	Employment of Disabled People → Details, Realization, Change
13.	Positive Media Representation → Backstory, Moment, Resolution
2. Draft Interview Questions
To guide each storyteller’s narrative and provide structure, here are questions crafted to elicit responses for each storytelling step.
Storytelling Step 1: Moment
Purpose: Capturing a defining event or experience that shaped the interviewee’s perspective on disability.
•	Can you tell me about a specific moment that changed how you view disability?
•	Was there a time when you faced or witnessed a challenge that shifted your perspective?
•	Can you describe a turning point in your life or work when you realised something important about ability or inclusion?
•	What is a moment that stands out in your journey as especially significant?
•	Can you share a moment when your disability gave you a unique perspective?
Storytelling Step 2: Details
Purpose: Explore the sensory and emotional context of the memory to bring the story to life.
•	What do you remember most vividly about that moment?
•	Can you describe the people, places, or atmosphere surrounding the experience?
•	How did you feel in the moment, and what thoughts were going through your mind?
•	Were there any small details that stuck with you or felt particularly meaningful at the time?
•	How did your disability shape the way you experienced or understood that moment?
Storytelling Step 3: Realization
Purpose: Highlight the interviewee’s shift in understanding or new insight gained.
•	At what point did you realise this moment was important?
•	What did you learn about yourself or others during or after this experience?
•	How did this moment change the way you think about disability, inclusion, or ability?
•	Was there anything about this experience that surprised you or challenged your assumptions?
•	Was there a realisation about your own capabilities or identity that came from this experience?
Storytelling Step 4: Change
Purpose: Uncover how the realisation influenced the interviewee’s actions, behaviours, or beliefs.
•	How did this experience affect the way you approach similar situations?
•	Did this moment lead you to make any changes in your personal or professional life?
•	What actions or decisions did you take because of what you learned?
•	How did this experience shape your role in promoting or understanding inclusion?
•	How has this experience influenced the way you advocate for yourself or others with disabilities?
Storytelling Step 5: Resolution
•	How has this experience stayed with you or influenced your goals moving forward?
•	What would you like others to learn from your story?
•	How do you think this experience contributes to broader conversations about disability or inclusion?
•	If you could go back to that moment, is there anything you would do differently, and why?
•	How has this experience changed the way you see your disability as part of your identity or story?

3. Perception-Specific Draft Questions
Using the storytelling structure, here are tailored questions for each of the 13 perception change factors:
1. Non-disabled People Working Closely with Disabled People
•	Can you describe a time when you worked closely with someone who had a disability? What was it like working together?
•	Did anything surprise you or change your view about their abilities?
2. Focusing on Similarities Instead of Differences
•	When you were working or spending time with someone with a disability, what common interests or goals did you find?
•	Did you realize any unexpected similarities that helped you see the person differently?
3. Providing New Knowledge about Disability, Including Ableism
•	Can you recall an instance when you learned something new about disability or ableism? What impact did this new knowledge have on you?
•	How did this learning change the way you see or interact with others?
4. Getting to Know the Person Behind the Disability
•	Is there someone with a disability you got to know personally? What did you learn about them beyond their disability?
•	How did getting to know them on a personal level affect your perception?
5. Time and Opportunity for Reflexivity
•	Did you have time to reflect on an experience related to disability? How did reflecting on it change your understanding?
•	What did you realize during your time of reflection that you hadn’t noticed before?
6. Moments of Realization and Awareness
•	Was there a specific moment that shifted how you saw disability? What led up to that moment?
•	What were you thinking or feeling during that realization?
7. Engaging with Provocative Art
•	Have you experienced any art or performance that changed your view of disability? What did the artwork say or make you feel?
•	What lasting impression did this art or performance leave on you?
8. Witnessing a Disabled Person Demonstrating Their Capabilities
•	Can you describe a time when you saw someone with a disability excel at something? How did it affect your perspective?
•	How did witnessing their abilities challenge any assumptions you might have had?
9. Co-production and Inclusion of Disabled People in Leadership Roles
•	Have you seen or worked with someone with a disability in a leadership role? How did their leadership impact you?
•	What did their role show you about capability and potential?
10. Challenging Self-Limiting Beliefs in Disabled People
•	Have you seen someone with a disability overcome a personal challenge or limitation? What was that experience like for you?
•	Did their journey inspire or challenge you in any way?
11. Disability Awareness and Inclusivity Training
•	Have you ever participated in any training about disability awareness? What did you learn that surprised you?
•	How did the training change the way you approach or talk about disability?
12. Employment of Disabled People
•	Can you tell me about a time you worked with a disabled colleague? How did their skills contribute to the team?
•	Did working with them change how you view disability in the workplace?
13. Positive Media Representation
•	Have you seen any media representation of disabled people that changed your view? What about it stood out to you?
•	How has this representation influenced your understanding or empathy?

4. Iteration Based on Project Context
Based on the project’s focus on young children’s understanding of disability, educator perceptions, and Spirit of 2012 evidence, here are further refinements to ensure the questions are aligned with project goals:
•	For Educators and Parents: Include questions about changes they’ve noticed in children’s understanding and empathy toward disability.
•	For Children’s Perception Formation: Ask about instances when children showed curiosity or understanding about disability, particularly if they’ve expressed changes in perception.
•	Project’s Inclusive Language Emphasis: Add specific questions on how the storytellers’ understanding of inclusive language has evolved and impacted their communication.
